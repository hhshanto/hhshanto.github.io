Chunking strategies that actually survive production

Six months of retrieval evaluation, condensed into the one finding I did not want.

Hasan Ibne Saleh 02 FEB 2025

Every team that builds a retrieval system arrives at the same place eventually: the embeddings are fine, the reranker is fine, the model is fine, and the answers are still wrong. The failure is almost never in the parts people enjoy tuning. It is in how the documents were cut up before anyone looked at them.

We spent the better part of last year trying to be clever about that cut. Semantic chunking, proposition extraction, recursive summarisation into parent-child hierarchies — each of them defensible, each of them expensive, and each of them beaten in our evaluation by a fixed window of roughly four hundred tokens with a fifty-token overlap.

The clever method wins the demo. The dumb method wins the quarter.

What we measured

Recall@10 against a hand-labelled set of 640 question–passage pairs drawn from real internal queries, not synthesised ones. Synthetic evaluation sets flatter semantic chunkers badly, because the generator and the chunker share a notion of what a coherent idea is.

PYTHON
def fixed_window(tokens, size=400, overlap=50):
    step = size - overlap
    return [tokens[i:i + size]
            for i in range(0, len(tokens), step)]

Four lines. It beat the pipeline that took a fortnight.
