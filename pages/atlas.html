Every piece on the site placed by what it is about rather than by where it is filed. --- {%- comment -%} The embedding atlas — the site's writing as points in a vector space. WHY THIS EXISTS ALONGSIDE THE CONSTELLATION. They answer different questions and disagreeing is the point. /constellation/ draws how the writing is FILED: a hierarchy the author chose, plus tags the author wrote down. This draws what the writing is ABOUT, measured from the text and nothing else. Where the two disagree — a psychology piece sitting among the politics, two posts in one folder landing at opposite ends — is information neither map gives on its own. NOTHING IS COMPUTED HERE. `.tools/embed.mjs` vectorises the corpus, runs PCA and writes _data/atlas.json; Jekyll reads that at build time and Liquid puts the coordinates on the nodes below. No model, no key and no request reaches a reader. The page is as static as every other page on the site — which is the whole reason the pipeline is offline. HONESTY ABOUT THE PROJECTION. The vectors have hundreds of dimensions and the screen has two. The three components kept here carry only part of the variance, and the figure is printed at the top rather than buried, because a scatter plot that has thrown away most of its information while looking authoritative is the most common way a visualisation lies. The neighbour lists are computed in the FULL space, not from the picture, so they stay true even where the projection flattens something. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign atlas = site.data.atlas -%}

    Built by .tools/embed.mjs, which reads the markdown, vectorises it, runs PCA and writes _data/atlas.json. It has two backends and the line above says which one produced this file. TF-IDF is lexical — pieces are close when they share words — and runs with no key and no network. The other calls an embeddings model and is semantic: it puts elections and ballots together because the model learned they belong together.
