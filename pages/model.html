A 500k-parameter decoder trained on this site and running in the browser. --- {%- comment -%} Screen: a transformer with the lid off. WHAT IS REAL HERE. Everything. A decoder-only transformer — token and position embeddings, pre-norm blocks, causal multi-head attention, GELU MLP, tied output head — trained by .tools/train.py on the twelve posts on this site and shipped as 489KB of int8 weights. assets/js/model.js reimplements the forward pass in about two hundred lines of plain JS, with no library and no WebGPU, because at 64 tokens by 128 dimensions the arithmetic is trivial. What makes real inference need a framework is the size of the matrices, not the difficulty of the maths. WHAT IS NOT. The output. 14,000 training tokens is nowhere near enough to generalise, so the model memorises: training loss 1.4, validation loss 9.5. That gap is the most honest thing on the page and it is drawn rather than mentioned. A reader who watches attention heads behave correctly while the text stays nonsense has learned the thing that no architecture diagram teaches — the mechanism is not the hard part. WHY THE 3D IS ONLY THE RESIDUAL STREAM. Because that is the only part of a transformer that wants three dimensions. A token enters as a vector and every layer ADDS to it, so its history is a path. Attention is a relation between two tokens and reads far better as bars; drawing it in the same scene would add an axis carrying nothing and cost the legibility of both. "The whole model in 3D" is not a visualisation — at 500,000 parameters it is a fog, and at GPT-2's 124 million it is a screensaver. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign m = site.data.tinygpt -%}

{%- comment -%} Loss curve, rendered at build time from _data/tinygpt.json. COLOUR. Two series, so identity cannot be carried by colour alone: both lines are direct-labelled at their right-hand end and the validation line is dashed as well. The pair is the site accent against plain ink — figure against ground — rather than two hues from the domain ramp, for two reasons. Those five hues MEAN something on this site (gold is Reflections, on every other screen), and reusing one here would be a semantic collision. And measured, they do not work: lifted for the dark ground they sit within ΔE 13 of each other in normal vision, under the 15 floor. Accent against ink measures ΔE 42 in light and 30 in dark. The two checks that pair does fail — a categorical lightness band, and a chroma floor that flags ink as "reads gray" — are both checks that assume a categorical ramp. Ink reads gray because it is ink. {%- endcomment -%}

Every token enters as a vector and each layer adds to it, so its history is a path, not a point. Dots grow along the path in the direction of travel: embedding, after attention, after the MLP, once per layer. The three axes are fixed — computed once over the whole corpus — and carry {{ m.basisExplained | times: 1000 | round | divided_by: 10.0 }}% of the variation, so this is a shadow of the real thing and not the thing.

Why it is this size

GPT-2 small is 124 million parameters — 500MB in float32, 124MB even quantised to 8 bits, past GitHub's per-file limit and far past what anyone should download to read a page. This is {{ m.paramsHuman }} parameters at {{ m.kb }}KB, using the same int8 quantisation that puts a 70-billion-parameter model on a laptop: one scale per tensor, weights stored as single bytes.

The second constraint was drawing it. A twelve-layer model would not be meaningfully slower in the browser and would be unreadable on screen — {{ m.nLayer }} layers and {{ m.nHead }} heads is what fits in a panel you can actually follow. That is also why the only 3D here is the residual stream. Attention is a relation between two tokens and belongs in bars; a scene containing every weight would be a fog, which is what "the whole model in 3D" always turns out to mean.
