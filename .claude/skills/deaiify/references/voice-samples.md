# Voice samples

Passages from Hasan's own posts where the template dropped away and the writing sounds like a person. Match these when rewriting.

**This file is a seed.** It was extracted from published posts, which are partly templated to begin with. It gets more useful as non-blog writing is added: notes, emails, messages, anything written without an audience in mind. Add to it whenever a sentence sounds unmistakably like him.

---

## Samples

> It's exhausting, this constant tug-of-war between hope and frustration.

> But then, reality hits.

> Then there are the days when I see another news story about corruption, another example of incompetence, another reminder of how slow progress can be.

> Some days, I wonder: will things ever really change?

> Corruption: It's everywhere. From small bureaucratic hassles to large-scale political scandals, it's hard to trust the system.

> Traffic jams, power cuts, pollution: small things pile up and make life unnecessarily difficult.

> So many problems exist simply because no one is held responsible.

> We've come so far since 1971, but issues like corruption, traffic, and economic disparity make it hard to feel optimistic all the time.

> I often find myself swinging between extremes when thinking about Bangladesh.

---

## What these have in common

**Sentences run short, and vary.** "But then, reality hits." is four words. The sentence before it is thirty. That contrast is doing the work; uniform mid-length sentences are the generated default.

**Concrete over abstract.** Traffic jams, power cuts, garments, 1971, another news story. When the writing is his, it names things. When it's templated, it reaches for "systemic challenges" and "structural barriers."

**Fatigue is allowed on the page.** "It's exhausting." He doesn't resolve it in the next clause. Generated prose can't sit in discomfort. It always reaches for the balancing insight.

**Questions to himself, not to the reader.** "Some days, I wonder: will things ever really change?" is genuine. Compare the rhetorical-question-as-transition move, which is a template device.

**Repetition as rhythm.** "another news story…, another example…, another reminder…" is a tricolon, but an earned one: it's cumulative and it's about exhaustion, not about balance. The tell in the catalogue is the *symmetric* tricolon of opposing pairs. This kind is fine.

**No summarizing.** These sentences never restate what the paragraph just said.

## What's absent from the real voice

- Section headers naming essay machinery
- Bolded list items with dash-clause explanations
- "In the end", "at the end of the day", "maybe, just maybe"
- Adverb intensifiers. The real sentences say "hard to trust", not "incredibly hard to trust"
- Em-dashes. The samples above are quoted with the punctuation the site now uses; the originals had em-dashes and they are banned outright (see tell #7)
